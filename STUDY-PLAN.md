# Study Plan

> Pick a route, protect focused study blocks, and spend at least as much time explaining mistakes as taking quizzes.

## Choose your route

| You already operate infrastructure | You are building foundations |
|---|---|
| Use the **16-hour fast track** | Use the **four-week foundation track** |
| Start with a timed diagnostic | Start untimed and build a glossary |
| Focus on unfamiliar product boundaries | Draw every architecture from memory |

## Fast track: 16 hours

Use this if you already understand servers, Linux, and basic networking.

| Session | Focus | Time | Deliverable |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Diagnostic + exam blueprint | 1 h | Weak-topic list |
| 2 | AI/ML/DL, training vs inference | 2 h | Explain each without notes |
| 3 | GPU architecture + software stack | 3 h | Rebuild the stack map |
| 4 | Compute, memory, storage, facilities | 3 h | Identify bottlenecks in scenarios |
| 5 | Networking, scaling, DPU | 3.5 h | Compare all data paths |
| 6 | Monitoring, orchestration, virtualization | 2.5 h | Tool-selection matrix |
| 7 | Timed practice + error review | 1 h | Final weak-topic list |

## Foundation track: four weeks

- **Week 1:** official fundamentals course; Domain 1 notes; glossary flashcards.
- **Week 2:** Domain 2 compute, facilities, storage; sketch a GPU node and rack.
- **Week 3:** Domain 2 networking and Domain 3 operations; perform the labs.
- **Week 4:** mixed practice, mistake journal, spaced review, and one timed 50-question simulation from an authorized provider.

## The mistake-journal loop

For every miss, record:

1. the concept tested;
2. why your choice looked plausible;
3. the cue that identifies the correct choice;
4. an official source that verifies it;
5. a new scenario in which the answer would change.

This converts memorization into discrimination—the skill most scenario questions require.

## Readiness checks

You are close to ready when you can:

- score consistently above your chosen safety threshold on fresh, legitimate practice material;
- explain why every distractor is wrong, not only why one answer is right;
- finish 50 questions with review time remaining;
- reproduce the cram-sheet comparison tables from memory;
- recognize when a statement is a common design tendency rather than an absolute rule.
